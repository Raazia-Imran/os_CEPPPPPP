\section{Background and Motivation}
Modern machine-learning inference systems may serve multiple applications, models or customers on a shared GPU. In this protocol, a \emph{tenant} is a separately managed inference workload or process whose performance may be affected by co-tenants. A single physical GPU may be shared to improve use of capacity and support more tenants; the magnitude of any benefit depends on workload and configuration and is an empirical question. GPU resource management involves driver/runtime and device-level behavior as well as operating-system scheduling. Hardware support for preemption and isolation varies by GPU and software stack; it must not be generalized from one product generation to all GPUs.

Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms} distinguishes the four assigned comparison families from related implementation labels. NVIDIA's documentation defines MPS as a service allowing multiple CUDA processes to share GPU resources, MIG as partitioning supported GPUs into instances, and time-slicing as a form of shared access with weaker memory/fault isolation than MIG in its GPU Operator setting \cite{nvidiaMPS,nvidiaMIG,nvidiaTime}. Vendor documentation establishes terminology and documented capabilities, not independent comparative inference results.

The motivation is a possible trade-off between utilization/tenant density and predictable per-tenant performance/isolation. It is a review hypothesis, not a result: some workload mixes may benefit from partitioning or sharing without the expected penalty, while others may show contention or stranded capacity. The relevant operating-systems concerns include time-sharing versus partitioning, throughput versus fairness, utilization versus isolation, and performance versus predictability. Contention can occur for compute, memory capacity, bandwidth, cache and scheduling opportunities; the causal mechanism and magnitude must be supported by individual studies rather than assumed.

Inference often has request-response behavior, so average latency may conceal slow requests. A reported p95 or p99 is the corresponding empirical latency quantile under a specified load, observation window and measurement method; it does not by itself prove a service-level objective (SLO) was met. Training-only behavior cannot be substituted for inference-serving evidence. GPU sharing should therefore be appraised under concurrent tenants, with workload, request rate, batching and baseline made explicit.

This topic is relevant to inference-as-a-service, multi-model serving and edge systems. Existing studies can differ in hardware, model, batch size, baselines and metrics, so a percentage from one study cannot automatically be compared to a number from another. Examples identified for search-strategy validation include PipeSwitch (temporal switching), REEF (preemptive inference scheduling), gpulets (spatio-temporal partitioning), and ParvaGPU (MIG/MPS spatial sharing) \cite{pipeswitch,reef,gpulets,parvagpu}. Their publication identities were checked against conference or publisher records; they are \emph{candidate seeds}, not studies selected by this review. A prior survey covers the broader multi-tenant inference stack and informs an overlap check, but is not a primary study \cite{yusurvey}. The protocol-led method follows software-engineering review guidance \cite{kitchenham}; PRISMA 2020 informs transparent selection reporting \cite{prisma}.

\section{Research Objective}
The main objective is to systematically identify, classify and synthesize peer-reviewed primary evidence on sharing one physical GPU among concurrent machine-learning inference tenants. The core comparison concerns time-slicing, NVIDIA Multi-Process Service (MPS), Multi-Instance GPU (MIG) and GPU API interception/remoting. GPU virtualization, custom scheduling and hybrids are coded as implementation layers or combinations when their inference evidence can be attributed to a core family.

The review will examine GPU utilization, tenant density, inference throughput, average/median and tail latency, predictability, cross-tenant interference, resource isolation and fault isolation. It will record the conditions in which each result was obtained, assess methodological strengths and limitations, and develop conditional guidance for engineers. A weak or absent comparison will be reported as a gap, not filled by presumed performance. The final primary-study target in the course guideline is approximately 15--20; this is a planning target, never a reason to relax eligibility or invent studies.

\section{Scope and Boundaries}
The unit of interest is sharing a single physical GPU for multiple inference tenants, even if the wider serving system uses several nodes or the GPU is accessed through an API proxy. A cluster paper can qualify only if separately reported same-GPU inference-sharing measurements can be extracted. API remoting is eligible only when the remoted endpoint is a shared GPU and its tenant-level effects are separable; mere remote execution is insufficient. Mixed training/inference work can qualify only if the inference subset is separable. GPU model serving, deep-learning inference, and related request-serving workloads qualify; training-only, non-GPU accelerators, pure CPU scheduling and cluster-only placement do not.

Eligibility is \emph{conjunctive}: a paper must meet the publication, mechanism, concurrent-inference, single-GPU evidence, evaluability and date requirements in Section~\ref{sec:inclusion}. A study does not qualify merely because it mentions one keyword. Surveys, position papers, vendor manuals and preprints may help vocabulary, references and contextual explanation, but are logged outside the primary-study set. A preprint with a later peer-reviewed version is resolved to the final version.

\section{Publication Period}
The formal main window begins 1 January 2020 and ends on each actual final search date in 2026, which will be recorded. This follows the guideline's recent-window preference and captures currently relevant MIG-era inference work without pretending that all foundational work began in 2020. To reduce historical bias for MPS, a prespecified targeted search (H1--H4) covers each database's indexed records before 1 January 2020, with no arbitrary lower year boundary; backward citation leads are logged separately. The historical lane is a discovery route, not automatic eligibility: an older peer-reviewed study may enter only through an individually logged exception if it introduces or establishes the evaluated mechanism, or supplies a directly relevant inference-sharing baseline that the main-window studies do not adequately document. Record discovery route, full-text evidence, specific need, decision by both reviewers and year. Apply IC1--IC5 and the same quality rubric. Tabulate older exceptions separately in the final study characteristics and sensitivity discussion. Older context-only references are not primary-study counts. An update near final submission should use the same protocol or a justified amendment. No study after a recorded cutoff is silently added.

\section{Screening and Study Selection}
Study selection will follow PRISMA 2020 reporting categories \cite{prisma}, retaining separate record, report and study identifiers, independent votes and reconciled counts.

\subsection{Seven-step workflow}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*:},leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Database searching.} Run each frozen string and record interface, exact query, filters, date/time and counts. Save raw exports untouched.
\item \textbf{Result export.} Export title, author, venue, year, abstract, DOI/URL and source-query fields where available. Assign immutable imported record IDs (R001, etc.).
\item \textbf{Deduplication.} First match normalized DOI; then review title, authors and year manually. Keep all source links, identify a canonical record, and record removed-before-screening counts. Do not conflate records with unique studies.
\item \textbf{Title screening.} Two independent reviewers mark include, exclude or uncertain. Keep uncertain records if title alone cannot establish ineligibility.
\item \textbf{Abstract screening.} Apply the same predeclared criteria independently to retained records. Record both initial votes and a short reason; resolve disagreement before full-text retrieval.
\item \textbf{Full-text screening.} Assign report IDs (P001, etc.), log retrieval attempts and PRISMA reports not retrieved. Two reviewers independently apply all criteria to available reports. Record one decisive EC code with explanation and page/section if excluded.
\item \textbf{Final selection.} Link eligible reports to unique study IDs (S001, etc.). Only eligible primary studies proceed to quality assessment, extraction and synthesis.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Decision register and reviewer agreement}
The spreadsheet will store immutable R/P/S identifiers; title, DOI, source database(s), source query/run, year and version; stage and reviewer; each independent include/exclude/uncertain vote; criterion, short justification and timestamp; adjudicator decision and reason; retrieval status and attempts; primary full-text EC code; and version links. A criteria pilot on approximately ten diverse records will identify ambiguous interpretations before large-scale screening. Each record receives two independent votes at title/abstract and full text; the third group member adjudicates unresolved differences. Preserve initial and final decisions rather than overwriting them. Consultation with the supervisor can be logged for unresolved scope issues, not used to invent eligibility.

\subsection{PRISMA count reconciliation}
From the register compute records identified by each database/other source, duplicate and other pre-screen removals, unique records screened, records excluded, reports sought, reports not retrieved, reports assessed, reports excluded by primary reason, and unique included studies. Check that identified minus pre-screen removals equals screened; screened minus excluded equals reports sought (adjusting explicitly for record-to-report links); sought minus not retrieved equals assessed; assessed minus excluded equals included reports; then map included reports to studies. If a report maps to more than one record or a study has multiple reports, disclose the mapping and reconcile counts. Counts are reported from the later executed search, not estimated in this protocol.

\section{Quality Assessment}
Ten criteria use a 0--2 scale: score \textbf{0} when absent/unclear, \textbf{1} when partly addressed and \textbf{2} when sufficiently supported. Maximum is \textbf{20}. A score is an appraisal of reporting and internal validity relevant to this review; it is not a publication prestige measure or an automatic inclusion gate. For each criterion record a brief rationale and source page/table/figure. Two reviewers score independently and reconcile differences, preserving initial scores.

Do not hide disagreement or aggregate dissimilar evidence mechanically.

\textbf{Comparison rule.} Separate within-paper head-to-head comparisons from cross-paper observations. Cross-study numbers are only juxtaposed in hardware, model, batch, concurrent-tenant count, load, metric and baseline strata sufficiently similar to interpret; otherwise describe qualitatively and explicitly state non-comparability. Explain conflicting findings with observed configurations and study limitations without speculating beyond evidence. Integrate the quality rubric and repeat key conclusions without low-quality studies to check robustness. A conditional decision matrix will list scenario/priorities, eligible mechanism, observed benefit, sacrifice, deployment constraint, alternative, evidence sources and confidence. It must permit ``insufficient evidence.'' No universal superior mechanism is assumed.

\section{Protocol Changes and Governance}\label{sec:changes}
Major changes after searching starts must be dated, justified and traceable. This includes changes to databases, search fields/terms, date range, eligibility, RQs, quality rubric, extraction or synthesis. Pilot refinements are also retained so the final query can be reconstructed. Editorial spelling and layout corrections do not constitute methodological changes. Search exports and earlier protocol versions must be preserved; changed eligibility requires re-screening of affected records and updated PRISMA counts.

\section{Protocol Summary}
This protocol defines a systematic review of GPU sharing for multi-tenant inference on one physical GPU. Four selected academic database interfaces will be searched with twelve logged main-window expressions and four targeted older-MPS expressions. The main primary-study period is 2020 through the actual final search in 2026, with individually justified older seminal exceptions. Two reviewers will independently screen and appraise peer-reviewed primary studies, log retrieval and disagreements, and construct a PRISMA 2020 flow. Structured extraction with page-level evidence will support a condition-aware narrative synthesis of utilization, density, latency, interference and isolation. Major changes after searching will be documented and justified.

\section{Introduction and engineering problem}
Concurrent inference tenants can share a physical GPU through temporal access, Multi-Process Service (MPS), Multi-Instance GPU (MIG), and API interception or remoting. The engineering decision concerns attainable GPU utilization or tenant density alongside response-time predictability and isolation. The effects depend on hardware generation, runtime configuration, workload mix and baseline; this review will evaluate those conditions instead of presuming a universal winner. The full problem definition and four-family scope are in the frozen Task 1 protocol. \pending{Replace this short context with source-anchored literature and the actual Task 2 required length.}

\section{Research objective and questions}
The objective is to identify, classify and critically synthesize peer-reviewed primary evidence about concurrent inference sharing of one physical GPU, including separable per-device evidence from larger systems. The review examines utilization, tenant density, throughput, mean and tail latency, predictability, interference, resource/fault isolation, and implementation costs.
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.]
\item What time-slicing, MPS, MIG and API remoting mechanisms are evaluated for concurrent inference on one GPU, and how do sharing granularity, layer and isolation boundary classify them?
\item How do studies measure utilization, throughput, density, average/tail latency, predictability and interference, and how comparable are their experimental methods?
\item Under what GPU, workload, batch, tenant mix and configuration conditions are changes in utilization, throughput, density and latency reported?
\item What resource and fault isolation properties, trade-offs, implementation costs and conflicting results are reported, and what limits the evidence?
\item Which approach or combination is supported for specified inference-serving conditions and priorities, with what confidence and alternatives?
\end{enumerate}
